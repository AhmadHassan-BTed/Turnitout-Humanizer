The Mathematics of Zero-AI Pattern Disruption

TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION

How do AI detectors like Turnitin AI, GPTZero, and CopyLeaks detect machine-written text? They do not "know" what was written by a human. Instead, they calculate two statistical variables: Perplexity (how predictable word sequences are) and Burstiness (the variation in sentence length and syntactic complexity).

"Because LLMs predict the mathematically most probable next token, their text has unnaturally low perplexity and flat burstiness. Turnitout breaks those exact mathematical distributions."

1. Contiguous n-Gram Disruption

Detectors examine 3-gram and 4-gram contiguous sequences against precomputed probability tables. When common cliché patterns like "delve into the comprehensive tapestry" appear, the sequence probability spikes to near 100%. TURNITOUT's 4,068+ rule catalog targets contiguous n-grams, replacing predictable tokens with valid, lower-frequency synonyms and alternating voice without altering semantic meaning.

2. PDTB 2.0 Discourse Connective Rotation

AI models over-index on specific formal transition words: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "In conclusion", "Additionally". TURNITOUT rotates connectives using the Penn Discourse Treebank (PDTB 2.0) taxonomy, swapping rigid transitions with conversational or varied academic connectors to elevate natural sentence burstiness.

3. In-Place XML Run Parsing (.docx & .tex)

Traditional tools flatten text into plain ASCII, destroying formatting. TURNITOUT parses Word documents at the w:r (XML run) level. Text transformations are mapped directly into existing character nodes, keeping bold, italic, font family, color, hyperlinks, math matrices, and XML headers 100% intact.

4. AST String-Aware Comment Tokenizer

In programming code, natural language only exists inside comments and docstrings. TURNITOUT uses a character-by-character scanner that tracks string boundaries (e.g. print("# not a comment")) and isolates comments (#, //, /* */). All executable statements remain completely untouched, ensuring 100% executable safety.
